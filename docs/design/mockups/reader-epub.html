Chapter 14

The Disappearance of the Perfumes

On an unnamed island off an unnamed coast, objects are disappearing: first ribbons, then emeralds, birds, roses—until things become much more serious. Most of the island’s inhabitants are entirely oblivious to these changes, while those few permitted to remember live in constant fear of the draconian Memory Police.

When the morning arrived, the breeze carrying over the bay had lost its familiar scent. It was subtle at first—like walking into an old apothecary shop where the amber glass vials had been washed clean with river water and set upon wooden shelves to dry.

I reached into the cedar drawer where my mother had kept the crystal atomizer. The glass remained cool and heavy against my palm, yet the fragrance had dissolved into sheer air, leaving behind only the faint sensation of a memory trying to remember itself.

“They are gone now, aren’t they?” the old man whispered from the doorway, holding a bundle of freshly gathered firewood in his crooked arms. His voice was steady, bearing the quiet resignation of someone who had survived twelve prior disappearances.
